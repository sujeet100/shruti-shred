# Harmony for a heavy rhythm guitar over a raga (research, 2026-10-11)

Research pass (one subagent, web sources) on how a distorted metal rhythm guitar can stay full
and heavy while staying inside a Hindustani raga. Practitioner interviews on how fusion bands voice
their rhythm guitars were thin; theory, acoustics and Hindustani sources were solid. Inferences are
marked as such.

## Principles

1. **The raga decides the harmony.** Every held pitch comes from the raga's own notes (Morris, AAWM
   11.1, 2023 — covers Hindustani and Carnatic; Ravikiran's melharmony — Carnatic-rooted). Varjya notes
   are out; vivadi only sparingly and in a set way.
2. **Legal notes are not enough; respect the movement.** A fixed tone on a degree the raga oscillates
   clashes with that oscillation (melharmony; Morris). In Bhairav komal re and komal dha take andolan and
   are vadi/samvadi (Tanarang) — *inference:* never sustain a tempered guitar note on them under the sitar.
3. **Anchor on the drone, tuned like the raga's tanpura.** Pa-tuning for Bhairav, Ma-tuning for Bageshree
   (ma-dominated) per the Bakshi list (tosslevy.nl); McLaughlin: the fundamental recolours the raga, so keep
   coming home to Sa (The Tonearm).
4. **Heaviness = fifths, fourths, octaves; thirds turn to mud** under distortion (Walser via Wikipedia;
   Mullin & Leinweber, arXiv 2504.04919; Lilja 2015). The fourth is a perfect consonance in Indian practice too.
5. **Pedal + dyads:** an open-string Sa pedal with legal fifths/fourths/octaves stabbed over it — standard
   metal vocabulary (Premier Guitar, Guitar World, MusicRadar); MusicRadar pairs E Phrygian dominant
   (Bhairav's scale) with E5 and F5, both legal in Bhairav. Octaves are legal on any raga note.
6. **Behave like a sangat accompanist:** follow, answer in the gaps (sawaal–jawaab), never overshadow
   (Swarganga; sarangi.net). *Inference:* chug/stab while the sitar holds; unison/octaves on the pakad.
7. **Out-of-raga colour is a deliberate, rare event**, never a voicing default (McLaughlin, The Tonearm and
   Boston Globe 2023: "I know how far I can go bending the rules, but not break them").

## Contested / unverified

- An out-of-raga power-chord fifth as "timbre" vs "wrong note": **no source addresses it for raga fusion.**
  Subagent's unverified judgement: masked in fast palm-muted chugs, audible as wrong when sustained under a
  held sitar note.
- Distortion sum tones (5/2 of the root, a major tenth): a distorted Sa5 carries a faint shuddha Ga — relevant
  to komal-Ga ragas like Bageshree. Inferred, not verified by ear; "svayambhu gandhar" weakly sourced.
- Whether Indian music should have harmony at all: Parrikar (against); melharmony, McLaughlin (carefully, yes).
- Tanpura assignments (Marwa Dha vs Ni) and Bageshree's aroha/avaroha differ between sources.

## Bhairav (Sa = E: E F G# A B C D#)

- Legal power-chord roots: S (E5), r (F5), G (G#5), m (A5). Illegal: P (B5→F#), d (C5→G), N (D#5→A#).
- Legal fourth dyads: S–m, **P–S, d–r**, N–G — the fourths make the roots whose fifth is illegal playable.
- Idiom: E pedal + E5–F5 stabs (legal, textbook Phrygian-dominant metal). F5 holds komal re/dha: stabs, not pads.

## Bageshree (Sa = E: E F# G A B C# D)

- Legal power-chord roots: S, R, g, m, P, n. Illegal: D (C#5 needs G#).
- Home sonority: Sa–Ma / A5 (Ma tanpura, Ma vadi). G5 and D5 for colour; B5 and F#5 descent-only (Pa, Re).
